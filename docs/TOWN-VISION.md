# Town direction — 2026-09-07

Status: user-requested product direction; implementation candidate is separate from production acceptance.

## What this place is for

People should feel the organization before they read its tasks. The common space is the normal entrance, not a peripheral chat channel. People may work quietly, show an unfinished thought, say hello, bring music or a photograph, help someone unexpectedly, or spend time together without an agenda.

Priority: human presence and serendipity; then understandable durable work; then removal of incumbent plan limitations. Online presence never creates an obligation to respond. Absence, silence and leaving are legitimate choices.

The scene is an abstract, simple, real-time 3D neighborhood. Organizations have houses; shared plazas, parks and cafes connect them. These are permissioned places, not one globally public stream. Guests and former members may retain explicitly granted commons access without retaining former workplace access. No automatic guest/alumni migration is implemented.

A personal desk aggregates authorized work across places. Shared boards retain notes, decisions expressed by humans, and tasks. Information has a direct 2D route; movement must never be a tax on doing work. Spatial distance enriches social context but is never permission to read confidential data.

## Stable constraints

- Text first; optional voice is a later separately verified capability, not a required provider dependency.
- Default-open within deliberately provisioned permissions; closed work belongs in explicit separate places.
- No DM as the central workflow. This is a shared place, not a replacement for every private communication service.
- Self-expression through avatars and voluntary greetings; no fabricated coworkers or simulated social activity in production.
- Small shared activities are part of belonging, not compulsory engagement targets. No activity ranking, streak penalty or employer monitoring.
- Short-lived received text may be selected into private memory; explicit sharing is another action. No background audio or screen recording.
- Durable human work is not subject to the ephemeral buffer's expiry.

## Information model in this candidate

Place is the authorization and shared-record boundary; a task may carry a project label. A personal desk is a filtered view, not a new permission scope. Shared project identity across organizations, knowledge canonicalization, and a central decisions registry are still product decisions. Do not claim that storing a label has resolved those questions.

## The first release experience

Open into a common space, recognize a colleague, greet them without sending a meeting invitation, approach them, exchange a low-pressure line of text, save a useful line privately, explicitly share one useful note, pick up a task from the desk, and leave without losing work or being scored.

## What not to confuse

The existing workbench/pilot was a sound authority and persistence foundation. It was not proof that the desired social space existed. The new Town entry is additive and isolated; it does not erase the existing workbench, claim it is deployed, or secretly migrate old data. Old feature restrictions tied to a previous task are not product requirements forever, but safety invariants and evidence remain binding.

## Adoption evidence still needed

Do people return voluntarily? Does lightweight interaction happen without more interruption? Can a remote person understand the day's atmosphere? Can a colleague retrieve an important old note and act on the current task without reading a whole stream? Can a guest/alumnus participate without data leaking? These need observation with actual participants; passing automated tests is not adoption evidence.
